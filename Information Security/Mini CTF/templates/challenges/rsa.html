RSA Overview
RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman) is the most widely-used public-key cryptosystem. It relies on the computational hardness of factoring large primes.

Key Pair
Public key (e, n) is shared. Private key d is kept secret. Anyone can encrypt with the public key, only the holder of d can decrypt.

Why Small Primes Fail
Using p=61 and q=53 is trivial to break. Real RSA uses 2048-bit or 4096-bit primes. Factoring the product would take millennia with current computers.

The Math
The security depends on: it's easy to multiply large primes (n = p×q), but extremely hard to factor n back into p and q.
