Vulnerability Intel

Brute Force vs Dictionary Attack
Brute force tries every possible combination. Dictionary attack uses a wordlist of common passwords — much faster and more practical.

Why This Works
Despite warnings, humans choose weak passwords. The 10 most common passwords account for ~2% of all login attempts. Many admins use obvious choices like "admin" or "password".

Essential Defenses
✓ Rate limiting: Block IPs after N failed attempts
✓ Account lockout: Lock after 5-10 failures
✓ CAPTCHA: Slow down automated attempts
✓ Multi-factor auth: 2FA defeats password-only attacks

Tools Used
Professional attackers use Burpsuite Intruder, Hydra, or custom scripts to automate brute force attacks at scale.

NIST Guidelines
NIST SP 800-63B recommends: minimum 8 characters, no composition rules (let users choose easy-to-remember passwords), check against known breached password lists.
